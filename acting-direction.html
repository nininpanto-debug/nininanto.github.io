THE JOURNEY

The Chapter That Almost Ended My Dream

A story of a dream, a first film, losing everything, walking away — and choosing to begin again.

Sometimes, a dream doesn't die because you stop believing in it.

Sometimes, you stop because people make you believe that you should.

My love for acting did not begin in front of a camera. It began on school stages. I participated in dramas and mime performances, then started writing my own dramas, creating mime acts and directing them.

Years later, during hotel management, a teacher proposed making a short film about the lives of hoteliers and chefs. When the class was asked who was interested, nobody showed much interest except me. Because of the way I had created dramas and mimes, I was selected.

It was one of the happiest moments of my life. I had no camera and no laptop. A junior student helped us with a camera, and a classmate gave us his laptop. I trusted them completely.

My love for acting did not begin in front of a camera.

It began on school stages.

I was drawn to dramas and mime performances. At first, I simply wanted to perform. But slowly, something changed. I started creating my own dramas, writing scenes, developing mime performances and directing them.

The stage became more than a place where I performed. It became a place where I discovered who I was.

Those performances brought recognition, prizes and appreciation. But more than the awards, they gave me something far more important — the courage to dream. I began to believe that perhaps storytelling could become more than something I did on a stage. Perhaps one day, I could create stories through cinema.

The Opportunity I Never Expected

When I joined hotel management, I never imagined that one of the happiest moments of my early creative journey would come from a classroom.

The idea of making a short film was not mine originally. It came from a teacher. The teacher wanted to create a short film connected to the world we were studying — the lives of hoteliers, chefs and hospitality professionals.

The class was asked who would be interested. Nobody showed much interest. Except me.

My previous experience creating dramas and mime performances had already caught the teacher's attention. Because of the way I had created and directed those performances, I was chosen for the project.

I was selected. It was one of the happiest moments of my life at that time. Someone had seen something in me. Someone had trusted me with a story. And I wanted to prove that their trust was not misplaced.

I Had No Camera. I Had No Laptop.

But I had a dream.

I wrote the story. I developed the screenplay. I took responsibility for directing. I handled the camera. I brought classmates and teachers into the project. I started turning an idea into something real.

I didn't own a camera. So a junior student helped us with a camera.

I didn't have a laptop to work on either. So a classmate gave us access to his laptop.

At that time, I never imagined that the same laptop would eventually become the place where the dream would disappear.

Then Everything Started to Change

As the project moved forward, the atmosphere around me began to change.

Some classmates started making fun of me. They mocked the way I was taking the project seriously. They made me feel as though I was doing something foolish.

The more I tried to continue, the more I felt the pressure. It wasn't one conversation. It wasn't one argument. It was days of comments, talks, jokes and discouragement.

Little by little, they tried to make me believe that I should withdraw. That I should stop. That perhaps I was taking it too seriously.

And there were moments when I almost listened.

But every time I thought about leaving, something inside me said — Continue.

So I continued.

The Project Became Bigger Than a Project

Somewhere along the way, I began to feel that perhaps this could become something meaningful.

And perhaps that was where the problem began.

There was ego. There was insecurity. There were people who started thinking about what might happen if I succeeded.

The project was no longer simply about making a short film. For some, it became about me. There was a feeling that perhaps I would become bigger than them. That perhaps this small opportunity could become the beginning of something bigger for me.

Instead of encouraging the dream, some people wanted to stop it.

They tried different ways. They talked. They mocked. They discouraged. They tried to make me withdraw.

But I kept coming back. Every time I thought I was finished, I somehow found the strength to continue.

I Didn't Know How Far They Would Go

Eventually, it became clear that simply making me lose interest wasn't enough.

They needed to stop the project itself.

And eventually, they found a way.

The footage and data we had worked so hard to create were deleted from the laptop.

Everything.

The work. The scenes. The effort. The time. The memories. Months of work disappeared.

Not because the camera stopped working. Not because we ran out of ideas. Not because I decided to quit. But because the work itself was erased.

And the laptop that I had trusted to help me create the film became the place where that dream came to an end.

I Didn't Even Try to Recover It

That is something I still think about.

Today, with everything I know, perhaps I would have tried. Perhaps I would have searched for backups. Perhaps I would have tried to recover the deleted files. Perhaps I would have fought harder.

But I didn't.

At that moment, I was broken. I was disappointed. I was humiliated. And after everything that had happened — the jokes, the pressure, the attempts to stop me, and finally losing all the footage — I simply stopped.

I didn't try to recover the data. I didn't try to restart the film. I didn't try to fight for that project anymore.

I walked away.

And for a long time, I believed that I had walked away from cinema forever.

The Dream Went Quiet

Years passed. I moved forward with life. I buried that part of myself. I told myself that perhaps acting and filmmaking were simply a chapter that had ended.

Until one day, an opportunity came that I never expected.

One Small Role

I was offered an opportunity to work as a supporting artist.

It wasn't a lead role. It wasn't the kind of role I had once imagined for myself. It wasn't a grand comeback.

It was simply one small opportunity.

But sometimes, a small door is enough to bring you back to a life you thought you had lost.

Standing in front of the camera again reminded me of something I had forgotten.

I still loved this.

The dream wasn't dead. It had only been buried.

Beginning Again

I returned step by step.

From supporting artist... to actor... to assistant director... to short film director... to casting coordinator... and eventually to someone who could help other people begin their own journeys — an acting coach.

What I Learned

For a long time, I thought that deleted footage was the thing that ended my dream.

Today, I see it differently.

That project was destroyed. The footage was deleted. The opportunity was lost. And yes, I gave up for a while.

But the dream itself survived.

It waited.

And years later, it found its way back to me.

Maybe the people who tried to stop that project succeeded in stopping that film. Maybe they succeeded in making me leave cinema for a few years. But they couldn't permanently stop what was inside me.

Because a real dream doesn't always disappear when you walk away from it. Sometimes it waits for you to return.

“The deleted footage didn't erase my dream. It only delayed it.”

Today, I look back at that chapter differently. I don't carry it only as a painful memory anymore. I carry it as part of the reason I became who I am.

It taught me about people. It taught me about ego. It taught me about disappointment. It taught me what it feels like to have something you worked for disappear.

But most importantly, it taught me something about myself.

I can fall away from my dream. I can lose years. I can lose opportunities. I can even believe that the dream is over. But I can still begin again.

Today, I am still acting. Still directing. Still creating. Still learning. Still helping others. Still writing new stories.

Not because my journey was easy. Not because everyone believed in me. Not because I never failed.

But because, after everything, I chose to begin again.
